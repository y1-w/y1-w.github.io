Blackwell Ultra B300

A1 A · Instruction-driven — SIMT / vector–tensor processors

Blackwell Ultra is the mid-cycle refresh of NVIDIA's Blackwell generation: two reticle-sized TSMC 4NP dies (208 billion transistors) linked at 10 TB/s and presented to CUDA as a single GPU with up to 160 SMs, 640 fifth-generation Tensor Cores and 288 GB of HBM3E.

02Architectural analysis

Compute organization

Each die carries four GPCs (eight in total) for up to 160 SMs. An SM pairs 128 CUDA cores with four fifth-generation Tensor Cores and 256 KB of dedicated tensor memory (TMEM) that holds accumulators close to the matrix units.

Relative to Blackwell, Ultra doubles the special-function-unit throughput for exponentials (about 10.7 tera-exponentials/s) to accelerate softmax in attention, and raises NVFP4 throughput via the second-generation Transformer Engine.

Memory orchestration

The L2 cache is shared and fully coherent across both dies; its capacity is not stated in the cited NVIDIA article. Off-package memory is HBM3E driven by sixteen 512-bit controllers (an 8,192-bit interface) for 8 TB/s. Capacity is configuration-dependent; 288 GB is the maximum.

As in other A1 designs, shared memory and TMEM give kernels explicit staging buffers, but the hierarchy as a whole is hardware-managed.

Interconnect & system scale

NVLink 5 provides 18 links at 100 GB/s (1.8 TB/s bidirectional per GPU) and can form a non-blocking domain of up to 576 GPUs; GB300 NVL72 racks use 72. NVLink-C2C connects to the Grace CPU at 900 GB/s, and the host interface is PCIe Gen 6 x16.

Programming & scheduling

CUDA with dynamic warp scheduling; tensor work is expressed through CUTLASS/cuBLAS/cuDNN, Triton and TensorRT-LLM. An 800 GB/s decompression engine offloads data-loading work.

Why class A1

Warp-scheduled SIMT lanes with embedded tensor engines and a cache-centred hierarchy.
